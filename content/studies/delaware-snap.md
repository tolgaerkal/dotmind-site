# Studio study: Applying for food benefits (SNAP) through Delaware DHSS

*dotmind · November 25, 2026*

> **Studio study. Not a commissioned project.** dotmind made this on its own to show how our agent roles could work on a real public service. It uses only information that Delaware Health and Social Services (DHSS) and public benefits sites publish. No DHSS data, systems or staff were involved, and DHSS has not reviewed or endorsed it.

A family applying for food benefits shouldn't lose weeks to a missing pay stub. This study shows how dotmind's agents could help a household finish its SNAP application completely the first time, and hand caseworkers a file that's ready to decide.

## The service today

Applying online is already possible. The hard part is everything around the form: proving income, the interview, and waiting. Here's what the public sources describe ([DHSS SNAP](https://dhss.delaware.gov/dss/divison-of-social-services/snap/), [ASSIST](https://assist.dhss.delaware.gov/Common/AssistHome), [Form 100](https://dhss.delaware.gov/dhss/dss/files/Form100_Application_42016ENGLISH.pdf), [BenefitsCheckUp](https://benefitscheckup.org/program/nutrition_de_snap_program)):

1. **Screen.** ASSIST has a "Do I qualify?" screening tool.
2. **Apply.** Residents apply online through ASSIST, on paper (Form 100), or at a State Service Center. The online application takes about 20 to 45 minutes and is available in English, Spanish and Vietnamese.
3. **Filing date.** For food benefits, the date DHSS receives the first page with a name, address and signature sets when benefits can start.
4. **Interview.** Every applicant completes an eligibility interview.
5. **Verify.** Common documents include photo ID, proof of residence, Social Security numbers for the household, recent pay stubs, and housing and childcare costs ([Feeding America summary](https://feedam.org/apply-snap/de)).
6. **Decision.** Under federal SNAP rules, a decision is due within 30 days, or 7 days for households that qualify for expedited help.

**Where people get stuck** (inferred from the process, not measured):

- **Missing documents.** The application is submitted, then the case waits for a pay stub or rent receipt the household didn't know it needed.
- **Missed interviews.** A scheduled call comes from an unknown number, or at a time the applicant is at work.
- **Not knowing what's happening.** Between applying and the decision, households can't easily see what's still needed.
- **Unsure about eligibility.** People who would qualify don't apply because they aren't sure whether their income or household counts.

## With dotmind

ASSIST stays the system of record. The agents work around it so the application arrives complete. The journey below follows one household: Denise (a fictional example), who works part-time and has two kids, after her hours were cut.

1. **Ask (Guide).** Denise asks, in English or Spanish, "My hours got cut. Can I get help with food?" The Guide agent explains SNAP in plain words and runs the screening with her. It explains what counts as household and income, instead of leaving her to guess.
2. **Provide (Collect).** Before she starts the form, Denise gets a checklist built for her situation: last 30 days of pay stubs, her lease, childcare receipts, and Social Security numbers for everyone in the household. She photographs each one. If her income is near zero, the agent tells her she may qualify for the 7-day expedited track.
3. **Check (Verify).** The Verify agent matches each document to the form. For example: "This pay stub covers only 2 weeks. Add the other one." Or: "Your lease shows a different address than the form. Which one is current?" Gaps get fixed before she submits, not weeks later.
4. **Interview prep (Prepare).** The agent offers interview times that fit her shifts, tells her the caller ID to expect, and gives her a one-page summary to have on hand.
5. **Follow up (Communicate).** She gets plain-language status updates ("received," "interview booked," "waiting on nothing from you") and a reminder before her renewal is due.

## The staff side

The caseworker opens a case that is already complete, with the evidence linked, and spends the interview on judgment, not paperwork.

- **Case summary (Prepare).** Household members, income for the last 30 days with the source document linked to each figure, and expenses. It's laid out the way the eligibility screen asks for it.
- **Flags with evidence (Verify).** For example: "Two pay stubs show different hourly rates. Both are attached." Every flag links to the document behind it.
- **Expedited cases surfaced (Route).** Households that may qualify for the 7-day track rise to the top of the queue so they don't miss the deadline.
- **Draft notices (Communicate).** Plain-language letters that ask for missing items or confirm a decision. A caseworker edits and sends them.
- **What it doesn't do.** It doesn't calculate eligibility or benefit amounts on its own, and it doesn't approve or deny anything.

## Safeguards in action

- **A caseworker decides.** Every eligibility decision, benefit amount and denial is made by DHSS staff.
- **Applying is never blocked.** The agent never tells anyone they don't qualify or shouldn't apply. Anyone can submit the first page right away to lock in their filing date.
- **Only necessary data.** It collects only what SNAP requires, and documents go into the agency's own system, not dotmind's.
- **Reasons are visible.** Every flag shows the document and the rule behind it, so the caseworker and the applicant both see why.
- **Language and access.** It works in English, Spanish and Vietnamese, on a phone or with a State Service Center worker. Phone and in-person options stay available.
- **Watched for fairness.** A pilot would check whether flags and expedited routing fall evenly across languages and household types.

## Expected impact and what a pilot would measure

We don't have DHSS data, so we aren't claiming any numbers. These are the hypotheses a pilot would test with one State Service Center and a limited group of applicants.

| Measure | How a pilot would track it | Hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applications complete at submission | Requests for missing documents per case | Fewer requests for pre-checked applications |
| Days from application to decision | Case timestamps | Shorter, especially for expedited cases |
| Missed interviews | No-shows vs. booked interviews | Lower when applicants pick their own time |
| Caseworker time per case | Self-reported plus system time | Less time gathering, more on judgment |
| Fairness | Outcomes by language and household type | No group is flagged or routed differently |

A pilot would follow the stages on our site: 2 weeks of discovery with caseworkers, 6 weeks of prototyping on anonymized cases, then 3 months alongside the current process with every case fully reviewed by a person.
